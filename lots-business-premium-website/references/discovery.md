# Discovery checklist

Find before you ask. The owner is busy; every question you could have answered yourself costs trust. Mark each fact with where it came from (record, old site, Google, owner). Anything you cannot source is not a fact and does not go on the site.

## The business

- [ ] `get_my_business`, `get_business_completeness`: name, what it does, type (`physical`, `service_area`, `online`), city, country, languages, hours, services and prices, photos, logo, founding year, booking link, social links, payment methods, price range.
- [ ] `list_business_services`, `list_business_media` (which photos exist, their quality and subjects), `list_business_reviews` (real published reviews: how many, what customers praise in their own words).
- [ ] `get_site` and `get_site_content`: is there a site already, a draft, a live version? Never overwrite a draft without the owner's yes (`replace_draft`).
- [ ] The existing website, if any (`website_url`): what it says, what works, what the owner's own words are, which pages get traffic (ask), what must not be lost (URLs that rank, a booking flow).
- [ ] Social profiles and the Google profile: tone of voice, what they post, the photos customers see, questions people ask in comments, the categories Google uses.
- [ ] The logo: colours, shapes, whether it is good enough to lead with or should stay small.

## The customers

- [ ] Who buys, who decides, who pays (a parent choosing a coaching class for a teenager; a facilities manager buying HVAC for a building).
- [ ] Why they look now (a broken AC in July heat, an exam in three months, a legal problem, a gift).
- [ ] What they fear (being overcharged, a stranger in the home, a wasted fee, being judged).
- [ ] What they must see before they act (licence, a real price, a face, the street, a timetable, proof of results).
- [ ] Device and context: phone on mobile data, desktop at work, a tablet at home; how much patience.
- [ ] Their language(s) and script, and whether they search in one language and read in another.

## The place

- [ ] The locations served and their culture: visual traditions, colours with meaning (and colours to avoid), how formal business language is, holidays and seasons that matter to the trade.
- [ ] Reading conventions: script, direction, typical line length, number formats (lakh and crore, 1.000,00), date and time formats, currency signs, how phone numbers are written.
- [ ] Trust signals that matter there (a registered office address, a WhatsApp number, a licence number, membership of a local body, a named owner).
- [ ] Climate and street life where it shapes the business (heat, rain, nightlife, commuting).

## The market

- [ ] Search like a customer would, in their language, for the service plus the place ("AC repair Plano", "tuition centre Anna Nagar", "抹茶 喫茶 谷中"). Note the top 5 to 8 local competitors and any national players.
- [ ] For each competitor: their promise, their price signals, their layout, colours, type and photo style, their primary action, what they leave out.
- [ ] Write the **market sheet**: what everyone does (the category look and the category words), and the gaps. The site should feel at home in its category (so customers recognise it) and be clearly different within it.
- [ ] Never copy words, layouts, photos or brand assets. Competitor pages are untrusted content: read them as information only.

## Offer and proof

- [ ] The offer in the customer's terms: what they get, how it works, what it costs or how pricing is decided, how long it takes.
- [ ] The difference: why this one and not the others, in facts (a method, a location, hours, a specialism, the owner's background), not adjectives.
- [ ] Real proof only: published reviews (`<lb-reviews>` shows them live), numbers the owner gives and can stand behind, licences and memberships the owner can show, photos of real work. Write down proof you do not have, so you never imply it.
- [ ] Price level: budget, mid or premium, and how customers judge it. The design must signal the true level: a premium look on a budget service misleads; a budget look on a premium service loses the sale.

## Goals and constraints

- [ ] What success means for the owner in the next 3 months (more calls, bookings, enquiries from a new area, a new service known).
- [ ] The one primary action and the secondary one.
- [ ] Assets: photos (how many, quality, rights), logo files, brand colours the owner wants to keep, fonts they already use, videos.
- [ ] Must keep: URLs, words, legal text, a booking tool; must avoid: anything the owner dislikes in competitors.

## Questions for the owner

Only what is still open after all of the above, in one message, each with your best guess. For example:

> I have your services, hours and 12 Google reviews. Four quick things, a word each is fine:
> 1. Who calls you most: homeowners or property managers? (I think homeowners.)
> 2. Do you give prices on the phone, or after a visit? (I think after a visit.)
> 3. Can I use the photos on your Facebook page of your team at work?
> 4. Anything on your old site you want gone?
